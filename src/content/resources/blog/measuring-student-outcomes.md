Most institutions already collect a remarkable amount of information about
their students. The trouble is rarely a lack of data. It is that the data
lives in a dozen disconnected places, each with its own owner, format and
update rhythm.

## The cost of scattered records

When a career fair sign-up sheet lives in one spreadsheet, mentoring notes
in another, and placement results in a third, nobody can answer a simple
question quickly: did the students who engaged with career support end up
better placed than those who did not?

Answering it means an afternoon of copy-paste, and by the time the report
is finished the term has moved on.

## Connect first, report second

The shift that makes reporting painless is not a better chart. It is a
shared record. When a student's activity is captured once and linked to a
single profile, the report writes itself. Participation, engagement and
outcomes become columns in the same table rather than three separate
research projects.

Start small. Pick one programme, connect its records end to end, and let
the first clean report make the case for the next one.
